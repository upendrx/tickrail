Waiting for the engine…

The pipeline, live

Every market event goes through these stages in order. The counters are running totals since start.

Stages 2 to 5 run on one CPU thread, the engine thread, so no locks are needed and every run is deterministic. Stage 1 (feed or simulator) and stage 6 (venue) run on their own threads and talk to the engine through lock-free ring buffers.

A stage's border lights up when its counter changes. Read left to right: many market messages come in, a smaller number of book updates reach the strategy, and far fewer orders go out.

Price and our quotes

Last 2 minutes. Hover to read exact values.

The grey line is the mid price, halfway between the best bid and best ask. The green and red lines are the prices where our buy and sell orders are resting. A market maker earns the gap between them when both sides fill, and loses when the price runs through one side (adverse selection).

PnL and position

Profit is marked to the current mid; position is in instrument units.

PnL = cash from all fills + position × current mid − fees. It moves even without trading, because an open position is revalued at every tick. Position (orange, right axis) shows inventory; a good market maker keeps it close to zero.

Depth chart

Cumulative size available at each price

Each curve adds up resting size moving away from the middle. A steep wall means lots of liquidity, so a large order would barely move the price. A shallow side is thin, and prices tend to move toward thin sides. That is also why the microprice leans toward the thinner side.

Full ladder

All levels the engine holds (up to 20 a side)

Red rows are asks (offers to sell), lowest at the bottom. Green rows are bids (offers to buy), highest at the top. The gap in the middle is the spread. Bar length is size relative to the largest level. Rows tagged "our quote" are prices where we have an order resting.

Strategy

—

Where the strategy wants to quote

Every price the strategy computes, on one axis in ticks from the mid

Read left to right: the market's best bid and ask, the strategy's fair value (microprice), the reservation price (fair value shifted away from our inventory), and the target bid and ask one half-spread either side of it. When we are long, the whole set shifts left so our ask is more attractive and we sell down the inventory.

Internal state

Parameters

Set on the command line when the engine starts

Our working orders

Orders the OMS believes are live or in flight

Pending new: sent, venue hasn't confirmed yet. Live: resting in the book. Pending cancel: we asked to cancel and are waiting for confirmation. Leaves is the unfilled quantity. Age is time since we sent the order.

Our recent fills

Executions against our orders, newest first

Maker means our resting order was hit; taker means we crossed the spread. Markout is how the mid has moved since the fill, in ticks, signed so that positive is good for us. Consistently negative markouts are adverse selection: we mostly get filled just before the price moves against us.

Limit usage

How close the engine is to each pre-trade limit

Every new order is checked against these limits on the engine thread, before it leaves, in well under a microsecond. Regulators require this (SEC 15c3-5, MiFID II RTS 6). Cancels are never blocked. Position usage counts the worst case: current position plus every open order on the same side filling.

Kill switch

Emergency stop

—

Pressing it sets one atomic flag. On its next housekeeping pass (within microseconds) the engine cancels every working order, and risk then rejects every new order. It also trips automatically when PnL falls below the max-loss limit. Resume clears the flag.

Latency by path

Rolling 30-second window of every measured event

p50 is the median; p99 means 99% of events were faster than this. In trading, the tail (p99, p99.9) matters as much as the median, because the slow moments are often the busiest and most valuable ones.

Timer resolution depends on the machine: on Apple Silicon the system counter ticks every ~41.7 ns, so small values land on multiples of that; x86 and most ARM servers resolve to a few ns. The multi-microsecond tail comes from the OS scheduler. Pinning the engine to an isolated core on Linux with wait = "spin" removes most of it (see the performance chapter of the docs).

How to run each mode
From the project folder, after cargo build --release

      

        
Live market data, paper fills

        
Real prices from a public feed. The engine simulates fills and never sends orders. Precision is looked up automatically.


tickrail run -c config/binance.toml
tickrail run -c config/okx.toml --set feed.symbol=ETH-USDT
tickrail run -c config/kraken.toml

        
Built-in exchange simulator

        
A full matching engine with synthetic traders, some of them informed, so market making here is realistically hard.


tickrail run

        
Backtest

        
Replays a journal or a CSV file through the strategy, deterministically, at millions of events per second.


tickrail replay data/binance-btcusdt.journal -c config/binance.toml --set strategy.half_spread_ticks=40

        
Analyse in Python


tickrail export data/binance-btcusdt.journal data/btc.csv
python research/analyze.py data/btc.csv

        
Every setting lives in the config file; --set section.key=value overrides one from the command line. tickrail list shows the compiled-in adapters and strategies.

Glossary

    

      
Bid / askBid is the highest price someone will pay; ask is the lowest someone will sell at.

      
SpreadAsk minus bid. Market makers earn it; takers pay it.

      
TickThe smallest price step the venue allows (BTCUSDT: 0.01).

      
LotThe smallest quantity step (BTCUSDT: 0.00001 BTC).

      
Mid(bid + ask) / 2.

      
MicropriceMid weighted by the sizes at the top. It leans toward the thinner side, where the price is more likely to move next.

      
Order book (L2)Total resting size at each price level. L3 would show every individual order.

      
Market makerPosts both a bid and an ask and earns the spread, taking the risk of holding inventory.

      
Maker / takerMaker orders rest in the book and add liquidity; taker orders cross the spread and remove it. Fees usually favour makers.

      
Post-onlyAn order the venue rejects rather than let it cross the spread, so it can only ever be a maker.

      
IOCImmediate-or-cancel: fill what you can now, cancel the rest.

      
Inventory skewMoving both quotes away from our position so trades tend to flatten it.

      
Adverse selectionGetting filled mostly by traders who know the price is about to move against you.

      
MarkoutPrice change after a fill, signed in our favour. The standard way to measure fill quality.

      
Tick-to-orderTime from receiving market data to sending an order. The core HFT latency number.

      
p50 / p99Median and 99th-percentile latency. The tail shows worst-case behaviour.

      
SPSC ringLock-free single-producer/single-consumer queue, the only way our threads talk.

      
JournalBinary log of every event, used to replay a session exactly.

      
Kill switchAn emergency stop that cancels everything and blocks new orders.

      
Paper tradingRunning the strategy on real data with simulated fills and no real money.
